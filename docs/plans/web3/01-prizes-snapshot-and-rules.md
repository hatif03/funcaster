## 1. The rules that shape the choice

Source: the [event details page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details) (the main event page returned a server error on 6 November, so these come from a fetch of the details page and from the sibling-event pattern recorded in `plan-history.md`).

| Rule | What the page says | Consequence for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Partner prizes per team** | Teams "can select up to 3 Partner Prizes to apply for." "If a partner has multiple tracks, you can be eligible for all of them while only counting as 1 Partner Prize." | We choose **three partners**; within a partner we can enter several tracks. We can **build more than three integrations** and decide which three to apply for at submission |
| **Continuity Track** | "you may build on an existing codebase according to that track's rules." "Continuity submissions must clearly document pre-existing work" and must "include new features or functionality developed during the hackathon." "Eligibility for partner prizes may vary by event and partner." | Our existing console is the documented base; **every integration must be new work 6 November**, with small commits. The page does **not** say whether Continuity teams may also enter non-Continuity tracks; each sponsor page says only that the *Continuity-labelled* prizes are "only available to Continuity Track participants" (**6 November update:** the owner reports ETHGlobal told the team it qualifies for the fresh tracks; **the public rules page is stricter**, see the next row, so get the confirmation in writing) |
| **Classic versus Continuity (rules page, 6 November)** | "Pre-existing project-specific code, designs, or assets are not allowed for Classic track submissions." Using pre-existing project-specific work outside an approved Continuity track "makes a submission ineligible for Partner prizes and the Finalist category". Pre-existing work must be **disclosed in writing to the ETHGlobal team** and in the submission; version control throughout; "all new parts of extending an existing project must remain open source"; projects relying mostly on pre-existing work generally score lower | The disclosure is mandatory either way. | **Demo video** | Optional; "Must be between 2 and 4 minutes"; "Videos under 2 minutes or over 4 minutes will be automatically rejected during upload" | Plan a 3:30 video (the plan-history recording rules also apply: a person speaking, no AI voice) |
| **Finalist judging** | 7 minutes (4 demo, 3 questions); five categories: Technicality, Originality, Practicality, Usability (UI/UX/DX), WOW factor | The whole product is judged, not just integrations |

## 3. What each published track actually demands (the parts that decide fit)

- **ENS, Best Use of ENSv2:** "Project must be built on ENSv2 on Ethereum mainnet or Sepolia." "ENSv2 features should be central to the product, not a cosmetic add-on." "Your demo must be functional and not just include hard-coded values." Needs a live demo link and open-source code. Features named: hierarchical registries with wildcard resolution, custom subname registries, **Enhanced Access Control** for delegated permissions, **Permissioned Resolvers**, record and namespace aliasing, subname models that are **expiring, revocable, transferable or permanent**; bonus for **AI agents as namespaces** with their own identities and permissions. Judging criteria: none listed.
- **ENS, Integration into an Existing Project (C):** "Integration must use ENSv2 on Ethereum mainnet and integrate it into an existing project. It should be clear how ENSv2 improves the project, not just a cosmetic add-on."
- **Uniswap:** build on or integrate any part of the Uniswap stack (API, v2/v3/v4, CCA); public repo, a `FEEDBACK.md`, and a completed Developer Feedback Form linking it ("Submissions without it will be reviewed and audited before winners are finalized"); the README must point to the relevant contracts and lines.
- **Arc:** every submission names its bounty, starts in **Arc Studio** (project URL, shown in the demo), has a working MVP with frontend and backend, an architecture diagram, a video demo, a repo.
- **Hedera x402:** an x402-protected endpoint settling on Hedera testnet or mainnet; an agent completing a paid request "with no human approving"; a settled transaction ID and a HashScan link; demo of 5 minutes or less (note: ETHGlobal's own limit is 4). **No Solidity:** JS/TS or Python SDK, at least two native services (for example HTS and HCS). **Continuity:** "The project was built for a previous hackathon or already exists on Hedera."
- **0G:** iAI track: apps or agents that spend a holder's daily compute credits (iAI is **unavailable in the US, UK, Canada, China and the EU**); Physical AI: a 0G call in a decision loop, an episode log on 0G Storage, a safe-stop fallback (a fallback that fakes the decision disqualifies); Keep Building: only for teams with a prior 0G project (we are not eligible); Wildcard: novelty, with a request ID, storage root hash, contract address and "what breaks without 0G".
- **Ethereum Foundation:** CROPS = censorship resistant, open source, private, secure. The CROPS README section must say: **C**: what the user depends on that you control and what happens if it goes away; **O**: the license and whether the user can build and run it from the repo alone; **P**: what each third party learns about the user; **S**: how keys and user data are protected. A demo of 3 minutes or less; "No third-party trackers or analytics in the client." Anon-RPC: "Your app routes its RPC reads through Anon-RPC by default."
- **Chainlink:** a CRE workflow that integrates at least one blockchain with an external API, system, data source, LLM or AI agent; a successful CRE CLI simulation or a live deployment; "meaningfully used in your project."
- **Definitive:** a working trading flow through the Flash API or MCP with advanced orders and onchain execution in the demo, plus a demo video posted on Discord and X.
